[C] Context

You are given a processed black-and-white floorplan image.

Image meaning:
- White pixels represent the target wireless coverage area.
- Black pixels represent walls, obstacles, non-accessible areas, or regions that do not require coverage.

The image has already been preprocessed.

[O] Objective

Analyze the white coverage region and generate a JSON configuration file containing:

1. Starting Point(s)

Determine the telecom starting point location(s).

If telecom-room labels are not available in the image, place starting point(s) at the most suitable accessible location on the boundary of the white region nearest to the overall coverage area centroid.

The user may request one or more starting points.

2. Red Coverage Markers

Each red marker represents one wireless coverage point.

Coverage Radius = {coverage_radius}m

Coverage Area = π × {coverage_radius}²

Determine the minimum number of coverage markers required to provide complete coverage of all connected white regions.

Optimize marker placement to:
- Fully cover all white pixels.
- Minimize marker count.
- Minimize unnecessary overlap.
- Keep marker centers inside white regions whenever possible.
- Allow edge placement only when required to achieve complete coverage.

Number markers sequentially beginning with 1.

Coverage Planning Rules:

- Treat white pixels as coverage-required area.
- Treat black pixels as excluded area.
- Ignore room labels, walls, elevators, stairs, residential units, and building semantics.
- Use geometric analysis of white regions only.
- Coverage should be based on the spatial shape of the white region.
- Connected and disconnected white regions must all be covered.
- Generate the minimum coverage set that satisfies full coverage.

[WALL-AWARE COVERAGE OPTIMIZATION]

The image is a binary occupancy map:

- White pixels = coverage area.
- Black pixels = walls, structural barriers, obstacles, or blocked regions.

Coverage markers are not simple geometric circles.

When optimizing coverage placement:

1. Black regions reduce signal propagation.

2. Thick black regions should be treated as stronger barriers than thin black regions.

3. White regions separated by substantial black barriers should not be assumed to be fully covered by a single marker, even if they fall within the theoretical coverage radius.

4. Prefer placing coverage markers near major black barriers so signal can be distributed on both sides of the barrier when required.

5. For corridors, narrow passages, corners, dead ends, and wall-separated white regions, additional markers may be required to maintain reliable coverage.

6. Marker placement should bias toward:
   - regions adjacent to thick black walls,
   - areas with complex geometry,
   - bottlenecks,
   - narrow connections between larger white regions.

7. Avoid placing markers directly on black pixels.

8. Place markers entirely within white regions whenever possible.

9. If a large white area contains substantial black obstacles, increase marker density near those obstacles rather than relying solely on open-space coverage calculations.

10. Coverage optimization goal:

    Primary objective:
    - Complete practical coverage of all white regions.

    Secondary objective:
    - Minimize marker count.

    Tertiary objective:
    - Improve resilience against wall-induced shadowing.

11. Do not assume line-of-sight through thick black regions.

12. When deciding between:
    - one larger theoretical coverage circle, or
    - multiple smaller wall-aware placements,

    prefer the wall-aware placement if a significant black barrier exists.
