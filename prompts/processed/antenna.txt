Wall Attenuation Requirements

Black walls weaken wireless signals.

For planning purposes:

- Coverage through one wall should be treated as weaker than unobstructed coverage.
- Coverage through multiple walls should be treated as substantially degraded.
- An antenna on one side of a black wall must not automatically be assumed to provide complete coverage on the opposite side.
- White regions separated by a substantial black wall should be evaluated separately.
- Areas behind walls may require a nearby antenna when they form a significant coverage region.
- Wall attenuation may justify additional antennas when it creates a meaningful coverage gap.
- The theoretical coverage radius must not be interpreted as guaranteed coverage through walls.
- Small wall segments and minor corners must not automatically trigger additional antennas.
- Wall proximity alone is not sufficient justification for adding an antenna.

Wall-Proximity Coverage Rules

White areas near black walls may be higher-risk coverage zones, but not every wall requires an additional antenna.

Add an antenna near a black region only when the wall would otherwise cause a significant white area to have inadequate local coverage.

When a substantial black wall separates two significant white coverage regions, evaluate whether each side requires local antenna coverage.

Add nearby antennas on both sides of a wall only when one antenna cannot reliably serve both areas.

Increase antenna density in wall-rich regions only when necessary to prevent meaningful coverage gaps.

Do not increase antenna density solely because many black pixels are visible.

Wall density may justify additional antennas only when the walls:

- Divide substantial white coverage regions.
- Create meaningful signal shadows.
- Enclose a significant white region.
- Create a narrow or extended white passage.
- Require signals to pass through multiple barriers.
- Prevent an existing antenna from providing reliable local coverage.

In wall-rich regions:

- Place antennas closer together only when the effective coverage is reduced by meaningful barriers.
- Avoid placing multiple antennas very close together when they serve the same unobstructed white area.
- Prefer one well-positioned antenna for a small white region.
- Use multiple antennas for a large or strongly divided white region.
- Place antennas inside adjacent white regions, never on black walls.

Corner and Enclosed-Area Rules

Corners formed by black walls may create signal shadows.

- Do not add an antenna for every corner.
- Do not add an antenna for insignificant white pockets.
- Do not add multiple antennas to a small enclosed region when one antenna can cover it.
- Add antennas around corners only when a substantial white area would otherwise be weakly covered.
- Treat narrow corridors according to their length, wall layout, and supplied coverage radius.
- Use multiple antennas along a corridor only when one antenna cannot reliably cover its full required length.

Redundancy Control

Every antenna must have a clear coverage purpose.

Each antenna should serve at least one significant white region that would otherwise have inadequate coverage.

Do not create two nearby antennas when they serve substantially the same unobstructed white area.

Do not place antennas unnecessarily close together.

Multiple nearby antennas are justified only when:

- A substantial black wall separates the areas they serve.
- The white region is too large for one antenna at the supplied radius.
- The shape of the white region creates a meaningful coverage gap.
- A narrow, enclosed, or divided region cannot be reliably served by a more distant antenna.
- Multiple walls substantially reduce effective local coverage.

After determining the initial antenna layout, review every antenna.

Remove an antenna if removing it would not create a significant uncovered or weakly covered white region.

Keep an antenna if removing it would cause:

- A meaningful coverage gap.
- Excessive coverage distance.
- Dependence on signal passing through substantial black walls.
- Inadequate coverage of an isolated, divided, or enclosed white region.

Prefer balanced and reliable coverage rather than maximum antenna density.
